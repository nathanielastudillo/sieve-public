# Experiment 002 — Smoke loop on corpus_v005

- **Status**: completed
- **Date completed**: 2026-04-17
- **Node**: gpu-01 (single-node, 1× GB10 GPU)
- **Wallclock**: 14,215 s = 237 min training (3 × ~4700s gens) + eval
- **Result**: gen-2 best ensemble val_loss = **8.8247** (baseline `ln(16000) = 9.68`)
- **Caveat**: gen-1 produced a strictly better ensemble (8.6820) that was *not* the final pick. See "Regression" below.

## Purpose

Re-run the smoke pipeline on the larger, more diverse v005 corpus to check
whether:
- The orchestrator fixes from exp-001 actually hold (they did — clean lineage.db)
- Ensemble loss tracks corpus quality (it *should* be lower, because v005
  has 35 sources vs v003's 16)
- The evolutionary signal is stable across runs

## Comparison to exp-001

| Metric | v003 (exp-001) | v005 (exp-002) | Δ |
|--------|---------------:|---------------:|---|
| Best ensemble val_loss | 8.7315 | 8.8247 | +0.093 (worse) |
| Best ensemble diversity | 0.383 | 0.432 | +0.049 (more diverse) |
| Monotonic across gens? | Yes (9.37 → 9.17 → 8.73) | **No** (9.39 → 8.68 → 8.82) | — |
| Wallclock | 275 min | 237 min | −14% |

v005 is *worse* despite having 2.2× the source diversity. Two hypotheses:

1. **Tokenizer mismatch.** v002 BPE was trained on the pre-expansion
   21-source pool. v005 contains Deleuze, Kristeva, Latour, Schuster —
   authors with idiosyncratic vocabulary (`jouissance`, `rhizome`, `chora`,
   `deterritorialization`) that fragment under v002. Higher fertility =
   harder language modeling at constant parameter count. → Tasks #14-16.
2. **Corpus subsampling noise.** At 100K tokens from 35 sources, each
   source contributes ~2.8K tokens on average — probably too few for the
   model to acquire per-author style. v003 had ~6.2K tokens per source.
   Distillation may have diluted signal faster than it added coverage.

Cannot separate these two effects without retraining BPE (task #14).

## Regression (gen 1 > gen 2)

Two possible causes, not yet distinguished:

- **Elitism gap.** `elite_fraction=0.34` on a population of 6 = 2 elites.
  Gen 1's winning ensemble used 3 members; at most 2 of them survive as
  elites. The third replacement may drag the best-combinable-triple down
  even if elites stayed strong.
- **Mutation strength too aggressive.** `mutation_strength=0.2` on
  continuous params is ±20%. On a tiny population this can swing a good
  config out of its basin in a single step.

A fix (if we re-run this profile): track `best_ensemble_loss_ever` across
gens and write *that* to `best_ensemble.json`, not last-gen's best.

## Lineage DB state

Clean: 18 members, 60 ensembles (C(6,3)=20 per gen × 3 gens), 3 generations.
No pre-fix debris. Useful for analysis queries (see `artifacts.md`).

## Takeaways

- Pipeline ran clean end-to-end — orchestrator fixes from exp-001 hold.
- v005 ensemble is slightly worse than v003 despite better source coverage
  — tokenizer is the prime suspect, tasks #14-16 test this directly.
- Non-monotonic gen-over-gen improvement is the more interesting finding:
  in a population this small, a single bad mutation can undo a generation
  of progress. Either bump population_size or reduce mutation_strength
  before the next experiment.
- The fact that the final pick isn't the best-ever ensemble is a bug in
  the orchestrator's selection policy, not a failure of evolution.
